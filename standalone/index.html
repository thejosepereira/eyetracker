VisionCorrect

Personalized computational rendering that pre-distorts a screen so a blurry eye perceives it clearly. Everything below runs in your browser.

Self-test viewer →

Enter your prescription and see the pre-distorted image live. Flip correction ON/OFF and judge it with your own eyes. Glasses off, one eye, held at the set distance.

Demo & blinded test →

Four-panel demo (original → what your eye sees → pre-distorted → corrected), plus a blinded 3-condition letter-identification study that scores results and exports CSV/JSON.

Dial to Clear →

One big slider. Glasses off, one eye — slide until the letters look sharpest, and that number is roughly your correction. Subjective refraction, screen-style.

Lab →

Many sliders + four side-by-side views (good-eye original, on-screen, bad-eye OFF, bad-eye ON) with numeric sharpness scores. For establishing baselines and calibrating the model against real prescriptions.

Distance-adaptive →

The front camera estimates how far your face is and the correction re-renders as you move. (Needs camera permission — this page's https makes that possible.) Manual slider works too.
Experimental R&D prototype — not a medical device and not a replacement for prescribed corrective eyewear. For accurate blur, set your real screen PPI and viewing distance. Source: github.com/thejosepereira/eyetracker.
